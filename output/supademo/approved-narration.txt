A novelist’s nightmare. One change, and the story stops making sense. Mara dies at the end of chapter two. So why is she still speaking in chapter three?

This is RETCON—backfill for stories. It’s an Apache Airflow plugin, with the writer’s workspace built right into Airflow.

Here’s The Aster Protocol, a five-chapter mystery. I’m changing Mara’s fate: she dies at the end of chapter two. First, let’s see what that would break.

Three later chapters mention her. Two still give her dialogue after she’s dead. That’s a problem. Chapter five only remembers her, so that passage is fine.

Now I apply the change. A character-canon Asset event triggers the repair workflow. I’ve shortened the model wait here.

Airflow uses dynamic task mapping to send only the broken paragraphs to its Common AI provider. The model proposes replacements; RETCON checks them. In this run, one needed a corrective attempt before it passed.

Then Airflow stops at its native human approval task. The original manuscript stays intact while the writer reviews the changes. Passing a check doesn’t make something good writing—the author still decides.

Approval resumes the Airflow workflow. RETCON publishes a new manuscript version, and Airflow records the published-manuscript Asset event. Three chapters checked, two paragraphs repaired, three chapters untouched.

And here’s chapter five. Sera still remembers Mara’s laugh. That memory belongs in the story. I can now export the revised manuscript as Markdown and keep writing.

That’s RETCON. One plugin, from a story change to an approved revision. Change the past. Keep your story whole.
